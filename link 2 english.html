THE MV WILHELM GUSTLOFF 1945

The Wilhelm Gustloff left the harbor on January 30, 1945.

Although the Wilhelm Gustloff was a ship built for 1,800 people, it departed with over 10,000 people on board.

The Wilhelm Gustloff was overcrowded and very cramped, making movement on the ship significantly more difficult.

On the night of January 30, 1945, the Wilhelm Gustloff sailed across the Baltic Sea; because of a code stating they were passing a German minesweeper, the ship had its navigation lights turned on.

At 9:15 PM, a torpedo from the Soviet submarine "S-13" struck the Wilhelm Gustloff. The torpedo left a large hole on the starboard side of the ship—a catastrophic breach.

But it was already too late for the Wilhelm Gustloff; the ship listed to the side and began to sink.

The Gustloff sank in just 50 minutes.

Due to the panic inside the ship and the overcrowding, over 50% of all passengers drowned before they could even escape the ship.

The water in the Baltic Sea was so cold at the time that those in the water died from the freezing temperatures within minutes.

Following this event, over 9,500 people lost their lives; only about 1,200 people survived.

To this day, the sinking of the MV Wilhelm Gustloff remains the deadliest maritime disaster in world history, with over 9,500 casualties.
